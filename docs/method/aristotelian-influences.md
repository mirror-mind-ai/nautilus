# Aristotelian influences

Some Aristotelian intuitions help illuminate the method: the passage from potency to act and the reading through the four causes.

These influences help explain why the method is interested in Value Generation, Realization, Human Territory, Evidence, Impact, and Integration.

## Potency and act

The distinction between potency and act helps understand what Nautilus calls Realization.

Potency is what may come to be. Act is what has become effective. An intention, possibility, idea, draft, conversation, or work energy is not yet full Realization. It carries potency.

When work gains form, touches the [Human Territory](human-territory.md), is sustained by [Evidence](evidence.md), and can be recognized by [Participants](participant.md), something passes from potency to act. Nautilus calls this passage [Realization](realization.md). Its orientation is [Value Generation](value-generation.md).

## Realization as passage

The phrase “effort becomes recognizable Realization” can be read through this key.

Effort alone is still in the field of potency. It may produce something, but it does not yet guarantee that something exists in the world of the Journey. Realization happens when the movement becomes effective: a Delivery becomes available, a decision stabilizes, a new capacity appears, or a change in the Human Territory can be recognized. Later reading, use, sale, or response belongs to [Impact](impact.md).

This is why Nautilus does not confuse activity with Realization. Activity may carry potency. Realization is recognizable act.

## From metaphysics to ethics

The first reading is metaphysical: what was possible becomes actual.

With Value Generation and Impact, the method also gains ethical orientation. Nautilus does not ask only whether something became real. It asks what value became real, for whom, and what consequences or responses appear after that availability.

This ethical layer does not make Impact controllable. It makes the Journey responsible for the value it seeks to generate and attentive to what the world returns after Realization.

## The four causes

The Aristotelian reading through the four causes also helps observe a Realization more completely.

A Realization can be probed through four questions:

- material cause: what context and materiality exist in the [Agentic Map](agentic-map.md) to sustain this Realization?
- formal cause: what form, structure, language, or organization makes this Realization recognizable?
- efficient cause: who or what produced the movement that led to the Realization?
- final cause: what value is this Realization for, what purpose does it serve in the Human Territory?

These questions do not need to appear as an explicit ritual in every session. They function as diagnostic lenses when a Realization is confused and as the basis of the [Mission Integrity Test](integrity-test.md).

## Relation to Nautilus concepts

The material cause appears in available context, Deliveries, artifacts, records, and concrete conditions of work. It asks whether there is enough materiality in the [Agentic Map](agentic-map.md) for the Realization not to remain abstract.

The formal cause appears in language, Mission, Delivery structure, and in the way Realization can be recognized.

The efficient cause appears in human and agentic Participants, actions, decisions, tools, and processes that moved the Journey.

The final cause appears in the Human Territory: the for-what of Realization, the value it serves, its consequence, usefulness, meaning, and contribution to the next turn.

## Relation to the Integrity Test

The [Mission Integrity Test](integrity-test.md) is the initial practical form of this influence.

A whole Mission needs enough context and materiality in the [Agentic Map](agentic-map.md), recognizable form, authorized movement, and situated finality. In Nautilus language, this means it needs to offer enough materiality for the work, give form to the intention, make clear which movement is authorized in this chamber, and explain why this matters in the Human Territory.

## Why this matters for agents

Agents tend to accelerate the efficient cause: they produce, answer, summarize, execute, compare, and generate artifacts.

Without form, evidence, and finality, this acceleration may produce only movement. Nautilus uses the grammar of Realization to ask whether the potency of work was actualized into something that exists in the Human Territory.

The Aristotelian influence helps preserve this question: it is not enough to ask what was done. We must ask what it is made of, what form it gained, by whom or what it was moved, and for what it came to exist.

## Synthesis

Potency and act help Nautilus think the passage from the possible to the realized.

The four causes help Nautilus observe a Realization by context and materiality, form, movement, and finality.

Together, these influences reinforce the method's central intuition: work only becomes Realization when it gains recognizable form and comes to exist in the Human Territory with finality, evidence, and capacity for continuity. What happens after that availability belongs to Impact and can be learned from without pretending it was fully controlled.
