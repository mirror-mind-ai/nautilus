# Participant

Participant is any presence that participates in a Nautilus Journey.

A Participant may have human or agentic nature. The concept allows humans and agents to appear in the same work grammar without erasing their differences.

## Why Participant exists

Nautilus deals with human-agentic work.

This requires a shared language to describe who participates in the Journey, formulates Missions, produces Deliveries, recognizes Evidence, sustains Realizations, and integrates learning. At the same time, this shared language must not pretend that humans and agents have the same nature, authority, or responsibility.

Participant is the term that opens this common space.

## Nature

The current version recognizes two initial natures:

| Nature | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Human | Participant with lived experience, situated responsibility, judgment, and final authority over human decisions |
| Agentic | Artificial Participant capable of supporting formulation, investigation, execution, synthesis, memory, and other delegated movements |

A Participant's nature informs how it may act, but does not yet fully define Role and Authority.

## Human Participant

A human Participant brings intention, lived context, values, decision, and responsibility.

In the method, humans are not merely task requesters. They participate in the Journey as sources of meaning, validation, authority, and integration. Even when agents expand production capacity, the human axis remains relevant for recognizing what matters, what was realized, and what should be preserved.

## Agentic Participant

An agentic Participant supports the Journey with artificial capacities.

It may help remember, organize, formulate, compare, generate artifacts, synthesize, propose next movements, execute delegated work, or support the collection and refinement of [Evidence](evidence.md). Its action must remain situated inside the Journey grammar, without replacing human authority where it is needed.

## Participant is not Role

Participant and Role are different concepts.

Participant identifies who or what is present in the Journey. Role describes a situated form of action inside a Mission or specific context.

The same Participant may exercise different roles throughout the Journey. Version `0.1.0-experimental` recognizes this distinction, but does not yet formalize Role.

## Participant is not Authority

Participation does not mean permission to decide everything.

Authority will describe who can authorize, validate, recognize, or close certain movements. The current version recognizes that the difference between participation and authority is necessary, but does not yet stabilize its grammar.

## Practical criterion

A Participant is well described when the Journey can answer:

- who or what participates;
- what its nature is;
- in which context it appears;
- what contribution it can offer;
- what limits of action must be preserved;
- what authority still needs to be made explicit before advancing.

## Boundary of the current version

In version `0.1.0-experimental`, Participant is a founding concept of the method language. Nature is recognized. Role and Authority remain future chambers.
