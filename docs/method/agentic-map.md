# Agentic Map

Agentic Map is the shared representation of the work context that allows human and agentic [Participants](participant.md) to understand where they are, what is at stake, which [Mission](mission.md) is being formulated, what materiality exists, what [Evidence](evidence.md) can be gathered, and what [Realization](realization.md) is being sought in the [Human Territory](human-territory.md).

It is not the Human Territory. The Human Territory is the real field where life and work happen. The Agentic Map is the navigable form of that territory inside the human-agent interaction.

## Why the concept exists

Agents do not access the Human Territory directly. They access representations of that territory in the form of conversations, documents, signals, instructions, artifacts, memory, tools, data, descriptions, and evidence.

Without a sufficient Agentic Map, the agent may answer fluently and still not understand where the work is situated. It may produce plausible outputs that are misaligned with the Journey, the available materiality, or the Realization being sought.

The Agentic Map exists to give ground to agentic action.

## Relation to Human Territory

[Human Territory](human-territory.md) is where Realization must make a difference.

Agentic Map is the operational representation that orients agentic action in that field.

The distinction matters: the map never replaces the territory. It can be incomplete, stale, distorted, or poor. That is why Nautilus requires [Evidence](evidence.md), human recognition, and [Integration](integration.md): the method needs to verify whether what the map oriented actually touched the Human Territory.

## Relation to material cause

In the [Mission Integrity Test](integrity-test.md), the material cause asks whether there is enough context and materiality in the Agentic Map to know what the Mission is made of.

This means the Mission must offer enough working matter: context, constraints, artifacts, data, people, situation, history, criteria, or signals. Without this, the Mission becomes too abstract and the agent may fill gaps with undue inference.

## What can compose the Agentic Map

An Agentic Map may include:

- Journey description;
- formulated Mission;
- involved Participants;
- relevant context and history;
- existing or expected Deliveries;
- available or possible Evidence;
- constraints and boundaries of action;
- decisions already made;
- open questions;
- Realization criteria;
- relation to the Human Territory.

## Map quality

A good Agentic Map does not need to contain everything. It needs to contain enough to orient the next movement without simulating certainty.

A map is insufficient when:

- the Mission seems clear, but the context is vague;
- the agent does not know which materiality to use;
- possible Evidence is not visible;
- the affected Human Territory is unclear;
- action boundaries have not been named;
- the agent needs to invent context in order to proceed.

## Synthesis

Human Territory is the real field. Agentic Map is the navigable representation that allows humans and agents to work on that field with sufficient context, materiality, limits, and direction.
