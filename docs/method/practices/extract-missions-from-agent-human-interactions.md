# Extract Missions from Agent-Human Interactions

## Practice intent

Extract Missions from Agent-Human Interactions is the practice of observing a real conversation between human and agentic Participants and recognizing whether a Mission candidate is emerging.

It makes the concept of [Mission](../mission.md) practical in agentic environments. The human does not need to begin by filling a fixed structure. The interaction itself becomes material for formulation.

## What the practice distinguishes

The practice distinguishes task extraction from Mission extraction.

A task is a unit of execution inside an assumed frame. A Mission is a unit of Realization in conduct. The difference is not whether there is intention, Delivery, or Evidence. A task may have all three. The difference is whether the interaction carries responsibility for the frame of value, authority, Participants, sufficient progress, Evidence, and Integration.

## Recognition question

The guiding question is:

**Is this interaction only asking for execution, or is it revealing a Mission candidate?**

A Mission candidate is present when the interaction makes the Participant ask:

- what Realization is being conducted;
- why it matters now;
- who participates;
- what authority is present;
- what tasks belong inside the frame;
- what Delivery or change would make value available;
- what Evidence would sustain recognition;
- what learning should be integrated.

## Practical flow

After a meaningful agent-human interaction, the Participant can pause and ask what the conversation revealed.

If the interaction contains only a local action, it may be recorded as a task. If it reveals a larger responsibility frame, it may be formulated as a Mission candidate. If several frames appear at once, the Participant can describe a Mission field instead of forcing everything into one Mission.

## Mission field example

A museum Journey may contain two simultaneous Missions.

One Mission is constructive: create a cinemateca feature in the museum product. Its Evidence may include a working feature, usable navigation, and the ability to publish or experience audiovisual pieces.

Another Mission is cadenced: sustain a daily publication rhythm for curated pieces. Its Evidence is not one isolated piece. Its Evidence is the continued rhythm of publication with sufficient curatorial quality.

A task such as preparing two pieces belongs primarily to the editorial Mission. At the same time, it may reveal product friction and generate learning for the cinemateca Mission or for the museum product more broadly.

## Output of the practice

The practice may produce:

- a task list, when only execution was present;
- a Mission candidate, when a responsibility frame emerged;
- a Mission field, when simultaneous Missions and shared tasks need to be seen together;
- Evidence candidates, when the interaction reveals how recognition may happen;
- Integration notes, when the interaction changes the next movement.

## Anti-patterns

The practice fails when it inflates every task into a Mission.

Not every request deserves Mission form. Some interactions are better preserved as tasks, notes, or Evidence. Nautilus should only formulate a Mission when the interaction needs coherence, responsibility, and recognizable Realization, not only execution.

It also fails when it forces the human to complete an ontology before acting. The practice exists because agentic work is conversational and emergent. The Mission is often discovered in the trace of the interaction.

## Boundary

This practice formulates and recognizes. It does not approve a plan, execute tasks, validate Delivery, recognize Realization, or close a Journey.
